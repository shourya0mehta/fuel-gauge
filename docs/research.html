Research, built with the Fuel Gauge pipeline 2003–2026

What fire cameras can see, and what their photos can't

Thousands of cameras watch Western hillsides for wildfire smoke. This project ties them to the ground to measure what they can actually see, and tests whether the same photos can tell how dry the brush is.

Shourya Mehta. Built only on public archives; code and data on GitHub, about the pipeline.

        One hillside, fifteen years

        A Forest Service camera above the San Jacinto Valley took a photo every day around noon from 2003 to 2018. Every photo here has been shifted, turned and scaled onto one shared view of the same slope, so the only thing that changes is the brush. Drag along the timeline to watch the chamise green up each spring and cure each fall.

        Why this is harder than it looks

        A camera on a pole is not a scientific instrument. Over fifteen years it gets bumped, re-aimed, replaced and fogged in, and its colours drift. Most of the work went into making years of photos comparable.

        Use it on your own cameras

        Everything above runs on an open Python package. Point it at a folder of daily photos from any fixed camera and it hands back registered colour measurements you can trust across years; give it a location and it ties each pixel to the ground.
